# Ghanaian cultural and period review

Status: **editorial review required before public release**

This is a review checklist, not a claim that any item is wrong. The comedy relies on Ghanaian speech, family dynamics, material detail, and a specific 1998 Accra setting. A Ghanaian editor with relevant regional and generational context should review the complete Ink script in play, not only isolated words.

## Language and usage

- **“Maaha” / “Yaa, me ba”** — confirm spelling, tones implied by context, who initiates the greeting, the appropriate response, and whether the exchange fits the time of day and relationship.
- **“Medaase”** — confirm spelling and whether the child's use, omission, and adult reaction sound natural.
- **“Agoo / amee”** — confirm orthography and the call-and-response context at Auntie Efua's door.
- **“Akola bone”** — inherited from the original prototype. Confirm spelling, literal/social meaning, level of harshness, who would say it, and whether it is suitable as a comic end rank.
- **“Palaver,” “chale,” “dash,” and “bofrot”** — check 1998 Accra usage, speaker fit, spelling, register, and whether explanatory context feels natural rather than instructional.
- Review all English dialogue for Ghanaian rhythm without turning speech into caricature. Check code-switching by character, age, and relationship.

## Money and shopping

Confirm that the old-cedi amounts and purchasing logic are plausible for the exact 1998 period represented:

- evaporated milk: **₵800**
- tea bread: **₵1,200**
- small packet of sugar: **₵900**
- matches: **₵100**
- toffee: **₵200**
- candles: **₵300**
- the **₵5,000 note**

Review the kiosk total, the accidental extra ₵1,000 change, the amount of bofrot it could buy, note design/availability, and whether a child would handle and describe these amounts in this way. If prices varied materially by month or neighbourhood, decide whether the script needs a less exact framing.

## Media, news, and public life

- **The GTV film rerun** — verify scheduling plausibility, channel naming, the kind of imported melodrama shown, reception quality, and family viewing behavior.
- **The 1998 Akosombo low-water power cuts** — verify timing, frequency, public terminology, and the relationship among outages, candles, kerosene lamps, radio, and television in Accra.
- **Daily Graphic** — confirm the publication name and how Daddy would refer to/read it.
- Check the television, antenna, aluminium-foil fix, CRT behavior, radio drama, and absence/presence of other household technology against 1998 urban household experience.

## Home and compound details

- **“GOD'S TIME IS THE BEST” kiosk slogan** — confirm that the phrasing, apostrophe, hand-painted presentation, and placement feel period-appropriate rather than generalized.
- **Padlocked phone dial** — confirm the mechanism, reason, household prevalence, and the child's likely understanding of it.
- **Kerosene lamps** — confirm the lamp design, fuel terminology, handling, smell/light references, and how children would interact with one during an outage.
- **The name Esi for Mummy** — confirm day-name implications and consistency with the character's background, age, and any unstated biography.
- Review the room dressing, clothing, kiosk inventory, compound layout, adult titles, greeting etiquette, biscuit-tray rules, errand expectations, discipline references, and neighbourhood gossip for specificity and respect.

## Character and family review

- Confirm whether Nana's name and presentation support the intended player flexibility.
- Check the relationships and forms of address among Mummy/Esi, Daddy, Auntie Efua, Sister Akos, Kojo, and Kojo's mother.
- Review Mummy's side-eye, Daddy's authority, Auntie Efua's hospitality, and the report-card labels to ensure the humour comes from recognisable people rather than flattening adult characters into tropes.
- Verify that consequences feel affectionate and socially credible, especially the “Akola Bone!” path and references to slippers or public embarrassment.

## Review method

1. Play at least one polite, one mischievous, and one highly strategic route.
2. Review every conditional callback in Episodes Two and Three, not just the default route.
3. Mark required changes directly in the Ink sources with the intended replacement and reason.
4. Recheck visual props and 2D/3D scenes alongside the corresponding lines.
5. After edits, run `npm test` and a complete browser playthrough so cultural revisions do not break tags, callbacks, prices, or report memories.

Record reviewer names, relevant context, decisions, and unresolved disagreements here before release.
